Financial Literacy Hub

Master the basics before risking your hard-earned money.

What is SIP?

Systematic Investment Plan allows you to invest small amounts regularly.

SIP is a method of investing a fixed sum regularly (monthly or quarterly) in a mutual fund scheme. It instills financial discipline and builds wealth over time.

By investing regularly regardless of market conditions, you benefit from Rupee Cost Averaging, buying more units when prices are low and fewer when prices are high.

What is an ETF?

Exchange Traded Funds track indexes and are traded like stocks.

An ETF is a type of pooled investment security that operates much like a mutual fund. Typically, ETFs will track a particular index, sector, commodity, or other assets.

Unlike mutual funds, ETFs can be purchased or sold on a stock exchange the same way that a regular stock can, offering greater liquidity and often lower fees.

What is a Mutual Fund?

A pool of money managed by professionals to buy diversified assets.

Mutual funds collect money from investors and invest the capital in stocks, bonds, or other securities. They are managed by professional fund managers.

They offer built-in diversification and professional management, making them an excellent choice for beginners who don't have the time or expertise to pick individual stocks.

What is Stock Market Risk?

Understanding the potential for losses in your investments.

Stock market risk, or systematic risk, is the probability of loss inherent to the entire market or market segment. It cannot be completely avoided.

Factors include economic recessions, political turmoil, or interest rate changes. While you can't eliminate market risk, you can manage it through diversification and long-term holding strategies.

What is Diversification?

Spreading your investments to reduce exposure to any one risk.

Diversification is the strategy of mixing a wide variety of investments within a portfolio. The rationale is that a portfolio constructed of different kinds of assets will, on average, yield higher long-term returns.

"Don't put all your eggs in one basket." By spreading investments across different asset classes (stocks, bonds, real estate, gold), you protect your portfolio against significant losses.

Investment Fundamentals

Master the key concepts behind your financial plan.

SIP

Systematic Investment Plan: invest a fixed amount monthly, benefiting from rupee cost averaging and compounding over time.

Lumpsum

Investing a larger amount at once. Best when you have idle funds. Works well alongside SIP for a hybrid strategy.

Step-Up SIP

Increasing your SIP amount every year (e.g., 10% annually). Small increases compound dramatically over a decade.

Inflation

₹50L today may need ₹90L in 10 years to have the same purchasing power. Always plan for inflation-adjusted goals.

Compounding

Returns generating further returns. ₹5,000/month SIP at 12% for 30 years = ₹1.76 Cr from ₹18L invested. Time is key.

Diversification

Don't put all eggs in one basket. Spread across asset classes — equity, debt, gold — to manage risk and smooth returns.
